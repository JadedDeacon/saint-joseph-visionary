## Introduction

Joseph was silent in the Bible. The Gospels record no spoken words from him. He appears in a handful of verses in Saint Matthew and Saint Luke, then disappears from the narrative. Yet those few scenes show a man who listened, decided, and acted. He protected the Child and His Mother. He worked with his hands. He obeyed the Angel’s warnings without delay. He is the model of a just man whose life was spent in service rather than speech.

This book follows the same format as the Visionary Bible. Each chapter of Part I pairs the canonical Scripture (Catholic Public Domain Version) with longer extracts from approved private revelations and mystical writings. The accounts of Saint Bridget of Sweden, Venerable Mary of Ágreda, and Blessed Anne Catherine Emmerich are given so that we may contemplate the same events from a different angle—not as a substitute for the Gospels, but as a help to enter more deeply into the silence and the action of Saint Joseph.

Part II gathers the principal traditional prayers, litanies, and practices that the Church has handed down through the centuries. These are offered so that the reader may open the book and pray. A short note on the earliest hymn addressed to Saint Joseph and the origin of Western musical notation appears as well, because the devotion that began in silence has been sung for centuries.

Private revelations are presented for edification only. They are not equal to Scripture and do not add to the deposit of faith. The aim of the book is simple: to learn more about the silent man of action so that we may follow his example.

## Part I: The Silent Man of Action — Scripture Paired with Mystic Accounts

### Chapter: Saint Joseph Prefigured in the Old Testament and His Parents and Tribe
Scripture supplies no direct prophecy naming Joseph, but the Davidic lineage is explicit. Saint Matthew 1:16 (CPDV): “And Jacob conceived Joseph, the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ.” Saint Luke 3:23 (CPDV) traces the line through Heli. The tribe is Judah through David.

**From Blessed Anne Catherine Emmerich (1774–1824), *The Life of the Blessed Virgin Mary***  
Joseph, whose father was called Jacob, was the third of six brothers. His parents lived in a large house outside Bethlehem, once the ancestral home of David, whose father Isai or Jesse had owned it. I saw the line of the descent of the Messiah proceeding from David and dividing into two branches. The right-hand one went through Solomon down to Jacob, the father of St. Joseph. I saw the figures of all St. Joseph’s ancestors named in the Gospel on this right-hand branch of the descent from David through Solomon. This branch has the greater significance of the two. Joachim was related to St. Joseph: Joseph’s grandfather Mathan had descended from David through Solomon. He had two sons, Joses and Jacob. The latter was Joseph’s father. When Mathan died, his widow married a second husband named Levi, descendant of David through Nathan. The fruit of this marriage was Mathat, the father of Heli, or Joachim. Joachim and Joseph were therefore cousins on the maternal side.

### Chapter 1: The Just Man and the Dream (Saint Matthew 1:18–25)
**Scripture (Catholic Public Domain Version)**  
And Jacob conceived Joseph, the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ. Now the procreation of the Christ occurred in this way. After his mother Mary had been betrothed to Joseph, before they lived together, she was found to have conceived in her womb by the Holy Spirit. Then Joseph, her husband, since he was just and was not willing to hand her over, preferred to send her away secretly. But while thinking over these things, behold, an Angel of the Lord appeared to him in his sleep, saying: “Joseph, son of David, do not be afraid to accept Mary as your wife. For what has been formed in her is of the Holy Spirit. And she shall give birth to a son. And you shall call his name JESUS. For he shall accomplish the salvation of his people from their sins.” Now all this occurred in order to fulfill what was spoken by the Lord through the prophet, saying: “Behold, a virgin shall conceive in her womb, and she shall give birth to a son. And they shall call his name Emmanuel, which means: God is with us.” Then Joseph, arising from sleep, did just as the Angel of the Lord had instructed him, and he accepted her as his wife. And he knew her not, yet she bore her son, the firstborn. And he called his name JESUS. (Saint Matthew 1:16, 18–25)

**From Saint Bridget of Sweden (c. 1303–1373), *Revelations*, Book VII, Chapter 35**  
Know most certainly that before he married me, Joseph knew in the Holy Ghost, that I had vowed my virginity to my God, and was immaculate in thought, word, and deed, and that he espoused me with the intention of serving me, holding me in the light of a sovereign mistress, not a wife. And I knew most certainly in the Holy Ghost that my perpetual virginity would remain intact, although by a secret dispensation of God I was married to a husband. But when I had consented to the annunciation of God, Joseph, seeing my womb increase by the operation of the Holy Ghost, feared vehemently; not suspecting any thing amiss in me, but remembering the sayings of the prophets, foretelling that the Son of God should be born of a virgin, deeming himself unworthy to serve such a mother, until the angel in a dream ordered him not to fear, but to minister unto me in charity. Of worldly things Joseph and I reserved naught to ourselves, except the necessaries of life for the honor of God, distributing the rest for the love of God.

**From Venerable Mary of Ágreda (1602–1665), *Mystical City of God*, Book II**  
While they were thus engaged in prayer the staff which Joseph held was seen to blossom and at the same time a dove of purest white and resplendent with admirable light was seen to descend and rest upon the head of the saint, while in the interior of his heart God spoke: “Joseph, my servant, Mary shall be thy Spouse; accept Her with attentive reverence, for She is acceptable in my eyes, just and most pure in soul and body, and thou shalt do all that She shall say to Thee.” At this manifestation and token from heaven the priests declared saint Joseph as the spouse selected by God himself for the maiden Mary. The divine pregnancy of the Princess of heaven had advanced to its fifth month when the most chaste Joseph, her husband, commenced to notice the condition of the Virgin. Saint Joseph awoke with the full consciousness that his Spouse was the true Mother of God. Full of joy on account of his good fortune and of his inconceivable happiness, and at the same time deeply moved by sudden sorrow for what he had done, he prostrated himself to the earth and with many other humble, reverential and joyful tokens of his feelings he performed heroic acts of humiliation and of thanksgiving.

**From Blessed Anne Catherine Emmerich**  
Our travelers arrived at the town of Bethlehem at four o’clock, a Saturday. As it was at the time of the winter solstice, the sun was already sinking and the night was falling. They entered the town, and wandered through many streets in search of a lodging-house or inn for staying over night. But having already applied at more than fifty different places, They found themselves rejected and sent away from them all. It was nine o’clock at night when the most faithful Joseph, full of bitter and heart-rending sorrow, returned to his most Prudent Spouse and said: “My Sweetest Lady, my heart is broken with sorrow at the thought of not only being able to shelter Thee as Thou deservest and as I desire, but in not being able to offer Thee even any kind of protection from the weather, or a place of rest.” About an hour had elapsed since the birth of the Child, when Mary called St. Joseph, who was still praying with his face to the ground. Approaching, he prostrated himself, full of joy, humility, and fervor. It was only when Mary had induced him to press to his heart the sacred gift of the Most High, that he rose, received the Infant Jesus in his arms, and returned thanks to God with tears of joy.

### Chapter: The Holy Death
**From Venerable Mary of Ágreda**  
Perceiving, in her exalted wisdom, that the day and hour for his departure from this cumbrous earth was very near, the loving Lady betook Herself to her blessed Son and said to Him: “Lord God Most High, Son of the eternal Father and Savior of the world, by thy divine light I see the hour approaching which thou hast decreed for the death of thy servant Joseph. I beseech Thee, by thy ancient mercies and by thy infinite bounty, to assist him in that hour by thy almighty power.” The last words which saint Joseph spoke to his Spouse were: “Blessed art Thou among all women and elect of all the creatures. Let angels and men praise Thee; let all the generations know, praise and exalt thy dignity; and may in Thee be known, adored and exalted the name of the Most High through all the coming ages; may He be eternally praised for having created Thee so pleasing in his eyes and in the sight of all the blessed spirits. I hope to enjoy thy sight in the heavenly fatherland.” Then this man of God, turning toward Christ, our Lord, in profoundest reverence, wished to kneel before Him. But the sweetest Jesus, coming near, received him in his arms, where, reclining his head upon them, Joseph said: “My highest Lord and God, Son of the eternal Father, Creator and Redeemer of the World, give thy blessing to thy servant and the works of thy hand; pardon, O most merciful King, the faults which I have committed in thy service and interactions. I extol and magnify Thee and render eternal and heartfelt thanks to Thee for having, in thy ineffable condescension, chosen me to be the spouse of thy true Mother; let thy greatness and glory be my thanksgiving for all eternity.” The most fortunate of men, saint Joseph reached an age of sixty years and a few days.

#### Ad te, beate Ioseph (Prayer after the Rosary)
Appended by Pope Leo XIII to the encyclical *Quamquam pluries* (15 August 1889).  
To you, O blessed Joseph, we have recourse in our affliction, and having implored the help of your thrice-holy Spouse, we now, with hearts filled with confidence, earnestly beg you also to take us under your protection. By that charity wherewith you were united to the Immaculate Virgin Mother of God, and by that fatherly love with which you cherished the Child Jesus, we beseech you and we humbly pray that you will look down with gracious eye upon that inheritance which Jesus Christ purchased by His Blood, and will succor us in our need by your power and strength. Defend, O most watchful guardian of the Holy Family, the chosen offspring of Jesus Christ. Keep from us, O most loving Father, all blight of error and corruption. Aid us from on high, most valiant defender, in this conflict with the powers of darkness. And even as of old you did rescue the Child Jesus from the peril of His life, so now defend God’s Holy Church from the snares of the enemy and from all adversity. Shield us ever under your patronage, that, following your example and strengthened by your help, we may live a holy life, die a happy death, and attain to everlasting bliss in Heaven. Amen.

#### Devotion to the Seven Sorrows and Seven Joys of Saint Joseph
**Suggested image (public domain):** Philippe de Champaigne, *The Dream of Saint Joseph* (1642–43, National Gallery). Wikimedia: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Philippe_de_Champaigne_(1602-1674)_-_The_Dream_of_Saint_Joseph_-_NG6276_-_National_Gallery.jpg
**Alternative for joys:** Georges de La Tour, *Saint Joseph the Carpenter* (c. 1642, Louvre). Wikimedia: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Georges_de_La_Tour._St._Joseph,_the_Carpenter.JPG


Traditional devotion (often prayed on the seven Sundays preceding the Solemnity of Saint Joseph). Structure: for each sorrow and joy, recite the corresponding prayer, then Our Father, Hail Mary, and Glory Be.

1. Sorrow: The doubt of Saint Joseph (Saint Matthew 1:19). Joy: The message of the Angel (Saint Matthew 1:20).  
O chaste Spouse of Mary most holy, glorious Saint Joseph, great was the trouble and anguish of your heart when you were minded to put away privately your inviolate Spouse, yet your joy was unspeakable when the surpassing mystery of the Incarnation was made known to you by the Angel. By this sorrow and this joy, we implore you to obtain for us the grace of surmounting all anxiety.  
Our Father. Hail Mary. Glory Be.

2. Sorrow: The poverty of the Nativity (Saint Luke 2:7). Joy: The birth of the Savior and the angelic praise.  
O most happy Patriarch, glorious Saint Joseph, your sorrow in beholding the poverty of the Child Jesus in His birth was changed into heavenly delight by hearing the angelic harmony. By this sorrow and joy, we beseech you that after this life we may hear the angelic praises.  
Our Father. Hail Mary. Glory Be.

3. Sorrow: The Circumcision (Saint Luke 2:21). Joy: The privilege of naming Him Jesus.  
O most excellent observer of the divine law, glorious Saint Joseph, your heart was pierced when the Precious Blood of the Infant Savior was shed, but the Name of Jesus brought you new life and heavenly joy. By this sorrow and joy, obtain for us that the merits of this Blood may be applied to our souls.  
Our Father. Hail Mary. Glory Be.

4. Sorrow: The prophecy of Simeon (Saint Luke 2:34–35). Joy: The prophecy of the rising of many.  
Saint Joseph, faithful partner in the mysteries of Redemption, the prophecy of Simeon foretelling the sufferings of Jesus and Mary caused you deep pain, but his prediction of the salvation of innumerable souls filled you with joy. By this sorrow and joy, obtain for us compassion on the sorrows of Mary and a share in the salvation Christ brought.  
Our Father. Hail Mary. Glory Be.

5. Sorrow: The Flight into Egypt (Saint Matthew 2:13–14). Joy: The overthrow of the idols.  
Saint Joseph, watchful guardian, great was your toil in the flight into Egypt, but great your joy in having God Himself always with you and in seeing the idols overthrown. By this sorrow and joy, grant that no earthly affection may occupy our hearts.  
Our Father. Hail Mary. Glory Be.

6. Sorrow: The return from Egypt and the fear of Archelaus. Joy: The safe return to Nazareth and the hidden life.  
By the sorrow of the return and the joy of dwelling safely with Jesus and Mary, obtain for us a life of quiet fidelity.  
Our Father. Hail Mary. Glory Be.

7. Sorrow: The loss of the Child Jesus in the Temple (Saint Luke 2:43–45). Joy: Finding Him again (Saint Luke 2:46).  
By the sorrow of the three days’ loss and the joy of finding the Child, obtain for us the grace never to lose Jesus and always to find Him.  
Our Father. Hail Mary. Glory Be.

#### Ancient Prayer for Protection
Traditional prayer of long standing (modern English form; often associated with early Christian devotion).  
O Saint Joseph, whose protection is so great, so strong, so prompt before the throne of God, I place in you all my interests and desires. O Saint Joseph, do assist me by your powerful intercession and obtain for me from your Divine Son all spiritual blessings through Jesus Christ, our Lord; so that having engaged here below your heavenly power, I may offer my thanksgiving and homage to the most loving of Fathers. O Saint Joseph, I never weary contemplating you and Jesus asleep in your arms. I dare not approach while He reposes near your heart. Press Him in my name and kiss His fine head for me, and ask Him to return the kiss when I draw my dying breath. Saint Joseph, patron of departing souls, pray for us. Amen.

#### Cord of Saint Joseph
**Suggested image (public domain):** Georges de La Tour, *Saint Joseph the Carpenter* (c. 1642, Louvre). Wikimedia: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Georges_de_La_Tour._St._Joseph,_the_Carpenter.JPG


Origin: Antwerp, 1657, after the miraculous cure of an Augustinian nun who wore a cord blessed in honor of Saint Joseph. Approved by the Sacred Congregation of Rites (1859) and enriched with indulgences by Pope Pius IX.  
The cord is of white thread or cotton, ending in seven knots (for the seven sorrows and joys). It is blessed by a priest and worn as a girdle under the clothing. Daily practice: recite the Glory Be seven times, once on each knot, together with the Prayer for Purity:  
O Guardian of virgins and holy father Joseph, to whose faithful custody Christ Jesus, Innocence itself, and Mary, Virgin of virgins, were committed; I pray and beseech you, by these dear pledges, Jesus and Mary, that, being preserved from all uncleanness, I may with spotless mind, pure heart, and chaste body, ever serve Jesus and Mary most chastely all the days of my life. Amen.

#### Thirty Days’ Prayer to Saint Joseph
Traditional devotion in honor of the thirty years Saint Joseph is believed to have spent with Jesus and Mary. Recite daily for thirty consecutive days for any special intention.  
Ever blessed and glorious Saint Joseph, kind and loving father, and helpful friend of all in sorrow! You are the good father and protector of orphans, the defender of the defenseless, the patron of those in need and sorrow. Look kindly on my request. My sins have drawn down on me the just displeasure of my God, and so I am surrounded with unhappiness. To you, loving guardian of the Family of Nazareth, do I go for help and protection. Listen, then, I beg you, with fatherly concern, to my earnest prayers, and obtain for me the favors I ask. I ask it by the infinite mercy of the Eternal Son of God, which moved Him to take our nature and to be born into this world of sorrow. I ask it by the weariness and suffering you endured when you found no shelter at the inn of Bethlehem for the holy Virgin, nor a house where the Son of God could be born. Then, being everywhere refused, you had to allow the Queen of Heaven to give birth to the world’s Redeemer in a cave. I ask it by the loveliness and power of that sacred Name, Jesus, which you conferred on the adorable Infant. I ask it by that painful torture you felt at the prophecy of holy Simeon, which declared the Child Jesus and His holy Mother future victims of our sins and of their great love for us. ### Chapter 2: Non-Written Traditions of Saint Joseph
The flowering staff appears in the apocryphal Protoevangelium of James and Gospel of Pseudo-Matthew (a dove emerges from the rod); the Golden Legend later adds the flowering detail. Ágreda’s account of the blossoming staff and descending dove at the betrothal is consistent with the tradition in spirit. Emmerich’s visions of the selection process also include a miraculous indication. The artistic lily-staff is a medieval development of this legend, not a scriptural detail. Local relic traditions (the staff in Naples, said to have arrived via England) lack a continuous early chain and are recorded as pious custom only. Joseph’s age is portrayed as mature rather than elderly in the fuller visionary accounts (Baij, Emmerich).

### Chapter 3: The First Hymn to Saint Joseph and the Origin of Musical Notation
The earliest widely attested hymn specifically addressed to Saint Joseph is the medieval sequence *Salve Pater Salvatoris*. It appears in manuscript sources from the fourteenth century onward and is sometimes linked to the circle of Adam of Saint-Victor or later medieval sequences in the same rhythmic style. Full Latin text (representative stanzas):

Salve, pater Salvatoris,  
Salve, custos Redemptoris,  
Joseph ter amabilis.  

Salve, sponse Matris Dei,  
Salve, hospes Jesu mei,  
Joseph ter mirabilis.  

Dulces cunae, dulces panni,  
Dulces dies, dulces anni,  
Dum visitasti Dominum.  

O quam fuit admiranda  
Tua vita veneranda,  
Portans Dei Filium.

English rendering:  
Hail, father of the Savior, hail, guardian of the Redeemer, Joseph thrice lovable.  
Hail, spouse of the Mother of God, hail, host of my Jesus, Joseph thrice wonderful.  
Sweet cradle, sweet swaddling clothes, sweet days, sweet years, while you attended the Lord.  
How admirable was your venerable life, carrying the Son of God.

**Origin of Western Musical Notation**  
The earliest practical Western notation for chant appears in the ninth century in the form of neumes (from the Greek *neuma*, “sign”). These were heighted or unheighted marks placed above the text to indicate the general contour of the melody. By the early eleventh century, Guido d’Arezzo (c. 991–after 1033) introduced the four-line staff and the solmization syllables (*ut, re, mi, fa, sol, la*), allowing precise pitch indication. The *Salve Pater Salvatoris* and similar medieval sequences were originally sung from neumatic or early staff notation of this type.

## Conclusion: Knowledge and Action

Knowledge is important, but action is probably more important. Saint Joseph shows us this with his life: he spoke no recorded words in the Gospels, yet he acted decisively at every moment God called him. Blessed Josemaría Escrivá, reflecting on Saint Joseph, taught that the ordinary work of a just man becomes the place of encounter with God:

> “St. Joseph was an ordinary sort of man on whom God relied to do great things. He did exactly what the Lord wanted him to do, in each and every event that went to make up his life. … Work thus becomes prayer and thanksgiving, because we know we are placed on earth by God, that we are loved by him and made heirs to his promises. We have been rightly told, ‘In eating, in drinking, in all that you do, do everything for God’s glory.’”

Love without action is meaningless. God does not need us to respond, but He wants us to respond with action. Even the devil knows some of the mysteries that we cannot comprehend. But he does not love God. And the way he chose to hate God was through his lack of service.

Saint Joseph’s silence was not emptiness; it was the silence of a man whose hands were busy with the work God gave him. May we learn from him to turn what we know into what we do.
